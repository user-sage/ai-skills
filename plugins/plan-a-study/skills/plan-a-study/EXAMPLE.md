# Example of usage: Plan a study

This example is illustrative. Anything in square brackets is for the person to supply. It contains no real participants, quotes or numbers.

## The request

> Plan a usability test for our new checkout. We keep losing people at payment.

## What the skill asks first

Before I write the plan, a few things will make it sharper:

1. Which decision will this inform, who makes it, and by when? For example, whether to ship the new payment step as it is.
2. Who are the customers who drop off? First-time buyers, returning buyers, mobile or desktop?
3. Is the new checkout live, or a prototype? A link helps.
4. What do you already know about the drop-off, such as analytics on where people leave, or support tickets?

If you would rather not answer now, I will write the plan with placeholders for these.

## The output

# Study Brief: Checkout payment step usability test

## Decision this informs
Whether to ship the new payment step as designed, or change it first. [Decision owner] decides by [date].

## Background
The team reports that people leave the checkout at the payment step. [Add the analytics source and the drop-off figure for the payment step, with the date range.] [Add any support tickets or past research on payment, with links.] No research on the new checkout has run yet.

## Research questions
1. Can people complete payment in the new checkout without help?
2. Where in the payment step do people hesitate, make errors or give up, and why?
3. What do people expect to see before they enter payment details, such as delivery cost, total price or payment options?
4. Do first-time buyers struggle in different places from returning buyers?

## Method and why
A moderated or unmoderated prototype usability test, with people completing a realistic purchase from basket to confirmation. The research questions are about whether people can do the task and where they struggle, so watching them do it answers them directly. The runner-up was a survey of people who abandoned checkout. It lost because a survey records what people say about payment after the fact, and it cannot show where they hesitate.

## Sample
Five to eight people per segment, which usually surfaces the main usability problems in a flow like this. Two segments, first-time buyers and returning buyers, gives [10 to 16] people in total. Screen for people who bought [product category] online in the last [3] months, on [device]. Recruit through [Your Panel, GenPop or both]. The screener will be written with the Write a screener skill.

## Timeline
- Week 1: sign off this Brief, write the screener and tasks, and run one pilot session.
- Week 2: recruit and run sessions.
- Week 3: analyse, and share the readout by [date].

## Roles
- Study owner: [name]
- Runs sessions or reviews recordings: [name]
- Notes: [name]
- Signs off the Brief: [name]
- Receives the readout: [product team and decision owner]

## Risks
- The prototype may not be ready for real payment entry. Use test card details, and tell participants not to enter their own.
- People may behave differently with no real money at stake. Ask them to buy something they would buy anyway, and watch for hesitation as well as completion.
- Two segments of five to eight cannot be compared as percentages. Report patterns and severity, and avoid percentage claims.

## What this study will not answer
- How many customers the new design will convert. That needs live analytics or an A/B test after release.
- Whether pricing or delivery costs drive the drop-off overall. That needs a survey of real abandoners or a pricing study.
