# Example of usage: Audit a study before launch

This example is illustrative. The study below was written to show common problems, and anything in square brackets is for the person to supply. It contains no real participants, quotes or numbers.

## The request

> Audit this study before I launch it. Be blunt.

The person pastes in a short survey with a screener:

- Research questions: (1) Why do people leave at the payment step? (2) What do people expect to see before they pay?
- Screener: "Have you shopped online in the last year?" Yes (qualifies) or No (ends the screener).
- Q1: How easy was our checkout?
- Q2: Would you pay with a digital wallet if we added one?
- Q3: What did you think of the delivery options and the price?
- Q4: Did anything confuse you? (open text)
- Q5: What is your favourite colour for our logo?

## The output

# Audit: Checkout survey

## Summary
Fix 4 items first, and consider 4 more.

Estimated length: about 2 minutes. Research questions checked against: why people leave at payment, and what people expect to see before they pay.

## Fix before launch

### Screener, question 1
Wording: "Have you shopped online in the last year?"
Issue: screener
Why it matters: Yes is obviously the answer that gets people in, so anyone who wants the incentive picks it.
Fix: "When did you last buy something online?" with "In the last month", "1 to 3 months ago", "4 to 12 months ago" and "More than a year ago, or never", and only the first two qualifying.

### Question 1
Wording: "How easy was our checkout?"
Issue: leading
Why it matters: It names only the easy side, so people anchor on it and your scores come out higher than they should.
Fix: "How easy or difficult was it to complete checkout?" on a scale from very difficult to very easy.

### Question 2
Wording: "Would you pay with a digital wallet if we added one?"
Issue: leading
Why it matters: It asks people to predict their own behaviour, and most will say yes to be helpful. The answer will not tell you whether adding it changes anything.
Fix: "Which payment methods did you consider using at checkout?" with the options you offer today, [other methods you are weighing], and "Other".

### Question 3
Wording: "What did you think of the delivery options and the price?"
Issue: unclear
Why it matters: It asks two things at once, so you cannot tell which one an answer is about.
Fix: Split it. "What did you think of the delivery options?" and then "What did you think of the total price?"

## Consider

### The whole study
Issue: method fit
Why it matters: Your first research question asks why people leave at payment, and a survey only collects what people say afterwards. It will not show you where they hesitate.
Fix: Add a prototype test of the payment step, and keep the survey for after the task.

### Question 4
Wording: "Did anything confuse you?"
Issue: unclear
Why it matters: It reads as a yes or no question but has an open text box, so many answers will be a single word.
Fix: "What, if anything, was unclear at the payment step?"

### Question 5
Wording: "What is your favourite colour for our logo?"
Issue: goal fit
Why it matters: It does not trace to either research question, so its answers will not help the decision.
Fix: Cut it.

### The whole study
Issue: goal fit
Why it matters: Nothing asks what people expect to see before they pay, which is your second research question.
Fix: Add "Before you entered your payment details, what did you expect to see on the page?"

## What I could not check
- The audience and planned number of participants. [Paste them in to check the sample.]

## Next step

Make these changes in the study in User Sage, then run the audit again. It is ready to launch when the summary has no fix before launch items left.
