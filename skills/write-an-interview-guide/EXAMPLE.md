# Example of usage: Write an interview guide

This example is illustrative. Anything in square brackets is for the person to supply. It contains no real participants, quotes or numbers.

## The request

> Write a 45-minute interview guide about how small retailers handle returns.

## The output

# Interview guide: How small retailers handle returns

Length: 45 minutes, remote. Participants: owners or staff who handle returns at small retailers that sell online. [Confirm the size range and sectors from the Brief.]

## Research questions
1. How do small retailers handle a return today, from the customer's request to the refund or exchange?
2. Which parts of returns take the most time or cost the most, and why?
3. What tools or workarounds do they use, and what makes them switch or give up on one?

## Coverage
| Research question | Interview questions that answer it |
| --- | --- |
| 1. How returns are handled today | 3, 4 |
| 2. Where time and cost go | 5, 6 |
| 3. Tools and workarounds | 7, 8 |

## Guide

### Introduction (4 minutes)
Thank you for your time. We are learning how small shops deal with returns, so we can make better tools. There are no right or wrong answers, and you are the expert here. With your permission, I will record the call so I can listen properly instead of taking notes. You can stop at any time.

### Warm-up (5 minutes)
1. Tell me a bit about your shop and what you sell.
2. What does your role look like on a normal day?

### How a return works today (12 minutes)
3. Tell me about the last return you handled. Start from when you first heard about it.
   - Probe: What happened next?
   - Probe: Who else was involved?
   - Probe: How long did it take from start to finish?
4. Was that return typical? Tell me about one that went differently.
   - Probe: What made it different?

### Where the time and cost go (10 minutes)
5. Walk me through the part of returns that takes up most of your time.
   - Probe: What do you do first?
   - Probe: What do you wish you could skip?
6. Tell me about a return that cost you more than you expected.
   - Probe: Where did the cost come from?
   - Probe: What did you do about it afterwards?

### Tools and workarounds (10 minutes)
7. What do you use to keep track of returns today? Show me if you can.
   - Probe: How did you end up using that?
   - Probe: What do you do when it does not cover something?
8. Have you tried something else for returns and stopped using it? Tell me about that.
   - Probe: What made you stop?

### Wrap-up (4 minutes)
9. Is there anything about returns we did not ask that we should have?
10. Thank you. [Explain the incentive and what happens next.]

## Leading-question check
| Original wording | Problem | Rewritten |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Would you use an app that automated returns? | Asks people to predict their behaviour, and most say yes to be helpful | Have you tried something else for returns and stopped using it? Tell me about that. |
| Don't you find returns frustrating? | Expects agreement, and names only the negative side | Walk me through the part of returns that takes up most of your time. |
| How do you handle returns and refunds? | Asks two things at once | Tell me about the last return you handled. Start from when you first heard about it. |

These originals are typical examples of the problem. [Replace them with any draft questions your team has, and run the check on each.]

## Notes for the interviewer
- Let silence sit for a few seconds before moving on. People often add the most useful detail then.
- Ask for the last real example whenever an answer turns general, such as "we usually…".
- If time runs short, drop question 4 and question 8 first. Protect questions 3, 5 and 7.
- Do not mention any product idea until the end, if at all.
